# 1. Project Proposal

## Problem statement

Industrial facilities cannot always use all the energy generated by their solar panels. Surplus energy may stay unused, and when generation is insufficient the facility has to take electricity from the grid. Solar panels are a more environmentally friendly source of energy, and facilities install them to reduce their carbon footprint, so every unused kilowatt-hour reduces that benefit.

## Target users

The system is meant for the people who manage industrial energy:

- Energy specialists at industrial facilities
- Power supply engineers
- Solar energy specialists
- Managers responsible for the facility's energy consumption

## Aim and objectives

Aim. Develop an intelligent decision-support system that maximizes the use of clean solar energy at the facility and gives the energy specialist clear recommendations, through efficient energy allocation and the use of battery storage.

### Objectives

- Define the system requirements.
- Develop a forecast of solar generation and facility consumption and an energy allocation algorithm.
- Implement the backend and database.
- Create a web platform with HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
- Develop a mobile application with Flutter + Dart.
- Test the system on various scenarios.
- Evaluate the effectiveness of the algorithm.

## Proposed solution and decision rules

The engineer sets the battery limits once: power, capacity, allowed charge range, starting level and daily cycle limit. The system then takes a facility file (solar panel power, facility load history and, if available, a solar irradiance forecast), checks it, and forecasts solar generation and facility load for the next day in 24 hourly intervals. Two calculations then run on the same numbers. The main one selects battery charge and discharge so that more solar energy stays at the facility and the battery limits are not broken. The second follows a simple rule. The output is a recommendation with a reason for every interval, which the energy specialist accepts or rejects. The system gives advice only and does not control equipment.

The system consists of a backend with a database, a web platform (HTML, CSS and JavaScript) and a mobile application (Flutter + Dart).

The simple rule is the second calculation and also serves as the baseline. Solar always supplies the facility first. Then, for every hourly interval, the rule works as follows:

| Situation | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Solar generation exceeds consumption | Charge the battery with the surplus |
| Solar is insufficient and the battery can cover the deficit | Use stored energy from the battery |
| Solar is insufficient and the battery has reached its lower charge limit | Import electricity from the grid |

Example with simulated values for one interval. Solar 80 kW, consumption 50 kW, battery 60% of 100 kWh. 50 kW of solar supplies the facility directly, and up to 30 kW of surplus can charge the battery, within its charging limit and current state.

An interactive web interface shows the file check, the daily balance, the 24-hour plan and the forecast error.

## Expected outcomes

- A working software system
- A forecast of solar generation and facility load, and an algorithm for allocating solar energy
- A web platform and a mobile application
- Visualization of the forecast, energy consumption and battery charge
- Test results and a comparison of effectiveness with a baseline scenario (the simple rule)

## Project scope, limitations and assumptions

The project is software only.

### Included

- Analysis of energy generation and consumption
- Forecast of solar generation and facility load for the next day (24 hourly intervals)
- Accounting for the state of the batteries
- Calculation of energy allocation
- Display of indicators and recommendations
- Testing on test or open data

### Outside the project

- Installation of real equipment
- Direct control of industrial devices
- Building own meteorological models (weather forecast data comes from the input file only)
- Integration with real industrial controllers at the first stage

### Limitations and assumptions

The system is tested on simulated or publicly available data, so no real industrial facility is needed.

It works from a facility file (history of generation and load, and a solar irradiance forecast when available) and forecasts the next day. Forecast accuracy depends on the quality of that data and is shown on screen when meter readings for the day exist. The one-hour interval is an assumption, because the specialist did not specify the data interval.

- Battery power, capacity, state-of-charge limits and the daily cycle limit are considered. Charge and discharge losses are not modelled.
- It produces software-based recommendations and does not control physical equipment.
- Effectiveness is assessed through simulation and comparison with a defined baseline. Improvements in real industrial operations are not claimed without real-world validation.

## Engineering complexity

The project combines data processing, algorithm design, software development, interface design, testing and performance evaluation. It has six engineering challenges:

- An architecture that processes multiple energy parameters
- An allocation algorithm that respects battery capacity, charge and discharge limits and demand
- Forecasting generation and demand under changing energy conditions and selecting appropriate actions
- An interface that presents measurements and recommendations clearly
- Verification through test cases and simulation scenarios
- Evaluation with measurable performance indicators

## Evaluation criteria and test scenarios

The system is evaluated on six indicators across four test scenarios, and results are compared with a defined baseline.

| Indicator | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Solar energy utilization rate | Share of generated solar energy used by the facility or stored for later use |
| Grid electricity consumption | Electricity imported from the grid in each test scenario |
| Surplus solar energy | Generated energy that remains unused or cannot be stored |
| Algorithm correctness | Decisions comply with energy balance and battery operating constraints, including the daily cycle limit |
| System usability | Users understand the energy situation and the recommendations |
| Forecast error | Difference between the forecast and meter readings for the same day |

Test scenarios: high solar generation, low solar generation, high facility demand, limited battery capacity.
