# 2. Evidence of the relevance

The relevance of the project was examined through a structured interview with a specialist, who answered four open-ended questions. The questions concerned the difficulties an enterprise faces when using solar energy, the use of surplus energy, the distribution of energy between solar panels and the grid, and the usefulness of a software tool together with the functions it should provide.

The specialist named the high cost of installing solar panels and the dependence of generation on weather conditions as the main difficulties. At the same time, the specialist considered that solar energy could help an enterprise reduce its electricity costs. Regarding surplus energy, the specialist stated that energy produced above the enterprise’s consumption should be stored in batteries and used when the panels generate less, while noting that the installation of batteries requires additional financial investment. As for the distribution of energy, the specialist argued that solar energy should first be used to cover the current needs of the enterprise because it is considerably cheaper than electricity from the grid, and that any shortfall should be compensated from the grid. Finally, the specialist regarded a software tool as useful if it clearly showed the enterprise’s consumption separately for solar panels and for the grid, as well as the amount of energy the panels generate per day. According to the specialist, such information would help to estimate the volume of surplus energy and to determine the number of additional batteries that may be required.

These statements informed the requirements of the system. The dependence of generation on weather conditions led to the requirement that solar generation be anticipated rather than assumed to be constant. The recommendation to store surplus energy in batteries led to the requirement that the system plan the charging and discharging of the battery. The principle that solar energy should first cover the current needs of the enterprise, with the grid compensating only for the shortfall, was adopted as the rule for distributing energy between the solar panels and the grid. The wish to see consumption separately for solar panels and for the grid, together with the amount of energy generated per day, led to the requirement that the system display consumption by source and daily solar generation. Finally, the interest in estimating the volume of surplus energy led to the requirement that the system report the amount of surplus energy.
